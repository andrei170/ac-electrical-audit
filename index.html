Finding 1 — Your Best Services Are A Bullet Point
Read from your live site, 3 September 2026
Services you offer 12 Pages built for them 1 Pages on the whole site 5 home, about, services, case studies, contact

Electrical installation. Maintenance. Lighting design. Smart home. EV charging. Fire alarm systems. Access control. Security. CCTV. HVAC. Thermal image surveying. Inspection and testing.

Those are not variations on one job. They are twelve different buyers with twelve different problems, and several of them are searched by name by people with budgets — a facilities manager looking for fire alarm servicing, a landlord who needs EICR inspection and testing, a business quoting access control.

A bullet in a list cannot rank for any of them. A page can. This is the single biggest gap on your site and the one with the clearest return, because the work is already yours — you just are not findable for it.

EV charging and thermal imaging are the two we would build first. Both are growing, both are searched explicitly, and most electricians in North Wales offer neither.

Finding 2 — Nothing Is Measured
Searched the full served page source
Google Analytics not present Google Tag Manager not present Google Ads tag not present Meta pixel not present Call tracking not present

Nothing at all. No way to know how many people reach the site, which services they read about, or how many of your calls came from it.

You told our team you are busy but open to more work. That is the position where measurement matters most — because the question is not “how do we get more enquiries”, it is “which enquiries are worth taking?” and you cannot answer that without counting.

Analytics is free and only records from the day it goes on. Every week without it is a week of history that cannot be recovered.

Finding 3 — The Template Is Still Showing
Default section labels visible in your live page
"Banner" appears 15 times "Introduction" visible "What We Do" visible "Our Partners" visible "Our Impact" visible "Our Programs" visible "Our Team" visible

Those are the default section names that come with the website template, not headings anybody wrote for you. “Our Programs” and “Our Impact” are charity and non-profit language — they have nothing to do with electrical contracting.

Individually trivial. Together they say the same thing as Finding 2: the site was built, handed over, and nobody has been back through it since.

It is a tidy-up, not a rebuild. But a facilities manager checking you out before putting you on an approved list will notice.

Finding 4 — No Proof Anywhere On The Site
The gap that costs you commercial work specifically
Star rating published none Review count none Customer testimonials none Accreditations shown none found Named clients none

You have a Case Studies page and no case studies visible on the homepage. That is the most persuasive material an electrical contractor can own, and it is one click away where most visitors will not go.

It matters far more for commercial than residential. A homeowner picks on reviews; a facilities manager or main contractor is putting their own name on the decision, and they look for evidence somebody else took the same risk first.

Accreditations are the other half of that. NICEIC, NAPIT, ECA, TrustMark, a Safe Contractor or CHAS registration — anything you hold should be on every page, because on commercial work it is often a pre-qualification requirement rather than a nice-to-have.

You describe yourself as “one of the leading electrical contractors in North Wales”. That is a claim about yourself. A named project and a logo are proof from somebody else, and they do a different job.

Finding 5 — Residential And Commercial Are Sold The Same Way
Where the money in your service list actually sits

Your own copy says you work “from residential environments to large scale commercial projects” — and the site then speaks to both in exactly the same voice, on the same page.

They are not the same customer. A homeowner wants a price and a date. A commercial client wants accreditations, insurance, method statements, references, and to know you can hold a programme. Nothing on the site is written for the second one.

Commercial and industrial is also the work that keeps a team of your size busy through the winter, and it is where fire alarms, access control, CCTV and thermal imaging all live. That is the page we would build first, and it is the one your competitors are least likely to have.

What Is Genuinely Good
The foundations are sound
  • Twelve services, named specifically. Not “electrical services” — thermal image surveying, access control, EV charging. People search the job.
  • A working enquiry form and a tap-to-call landline: 01492 549740.
  • Email on your own domain, which a surprising number of contractors still get wrong.
  • A Case Studies section already exists — the structure is there, it just needs bringing forward.
  • The site looks professional and works properly on a phone. It is not a cheap build.
  • EV charging and smart home are on the list — two genuinely growing markets that most local electricians have not moved into.
  • Ten years trading with a real team. That is the hard part and it is already done.

Nothing here needs rebuilding. It needs pages for the services you already sell, proof brought to the front, and something counting.
